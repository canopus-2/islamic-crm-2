# UK Mosque & Physical-Centre Islamic Charity CRM/Operations SaaS — Comprehensive Market Research (2026-02)

> Purpose: Provide market research + competitive landscape + UK-specific operational/compliance drivers for a Beacon-style product targeted at **mosques / Islamic centres / Islamic charities with physical premises** in the UK.

---

## 1) Market Definition & Scope

### 1.1 Target customer types (UK)

**A) Mosque charities / Islamic centres (physical premises)**

- Facilities-based organisations that deliver religious services + community programmes.
- Often structured as **registered charities** (unincorporated associations, CIOs, charitable companies).
- Revenue is strongly donations-driven, with recurring spikes around **Jumu’ah** and **Ramadan**.

**B) Islamic charities with physical centres**

- “Community hub” charities (youth services, welfare, education, advice) with an on-site venue.
- Similar operational needs (facility bookings, programme registrations, volunteer management) + fundraising.

**C) Multi-site organisations**

- One charity running multiple buildings (mosque + community centre + satellite prayer spaces).
- Often need consolidated reporting + per-location donation/GASDS handling.

## 2) Market Size & Demand Signals

### 2.1 Number of mosques / centres in the UK (directionally)

- Ayaan Institute’s _Mosques in Britain_ study references an estimated **~1,884 mosques** in the UK, and identifies **1,179** registered charity mosques (~62.5% of estimated total). [S1]
- Other reporting around the same report references an estimated **~1,844 mosques**, and many are charity-registered. [S2]
- Independently maintained datasets track UK prayer spaces/masajid (useful for prospecting), though counts vary by definitions (mosque vs prayer space vs rented rooms). [S3], [S4]

**Implication:** The TAM for “mosque-centred organisations” is plausibly **~1.5k–2k+ entities** depending on scope, plus Islamic charities with centres.

### 2.2 Operating model: volunteer-heavy = admin burden (strong SaaS pain)

- Ayaan Institute-based reporting indicates **most mosques are small-scale and volunteer-led**, and cites high volunteer participation (e.g., ~86% volunteer component in the mosque workforce in the dataset described). [S1]

**Implication:** Time-saving admin automation is an unusually strong selling point (treasurer/trustee time is scarce).

### 2.3 Muslim giving: strong donation propensity + Ramadan spikes

- Muslim charitable giving has been estimated at **~£708m annually** by referenced research; other studies indicate higher average giving among Muslim donors vs national average. [S5]
- Blue State’s _British Muslim Giving Behaviours_ report indicates high intent to give more, Zakat frequently split into multiple payments, and strong responsiveness to major humanitarian causes. [S6]
- UK regulators explicitly note Ramadan giving is significant among **~3.9m British Muslims**, reinforcing the seasonal fundraising context and scrutiny (“donate with confidence”). [S7]

**Implication:** Donation tooling + comms + trust + transparency features are central. “Ramadan mode” is a real product concept, not marketing fluff.

## 3) How Mosques & Physical-Centre Islamic Charities Operate (UK Reality)

### 3.1 Governance & decision-making

- Typically governed by **trustees / management committee**, often volunteers.
- Regular meetings; approvals for major spend; scrutiny from community stakeholders (“where did funds go?”).

### 3.2 Core operational workflows (facility-first)

**A) Facilities & premises operations**

- Maintenance, contractors, keys/access, safety checks, insurance.
- Security concerns increasingly relevant (funding schemes exist; heightened awareness post-2023/2024 events). (Context signal; not core to product unless building security workflows.)

**B) Programme delivery**

- Maktab/education, youth services, women’s circles, welfare advice, events, funerals support, etc.
- Registrations, fees, attendance lists, safeguarding processes (sensitive).

**C) Hall hire / bookings**

- Calendar management, deposits, invoices/receipts, terms & conditions, damage deposits.

**D) People ops**

- Membership, volunteer rotas, communications lists.

### 3.3 Money in (donations + payments) is multi-channel and “spiky”

Common channels:

- **Cash** collections (especially after prayers / Jumu’ah).
- **Contactless terminals / kiosks** on-site.
- **QR codes** on posters.
- **Online donations** (website) + recurring donations.
- **Standing orders / memberships**.
- **Fees**: maktab/classes, events, hall hire.

### 3.4 Money out (restricted funds + accountability)

- Utilities, rent/mortgage, staffing, maintenance.
- Restricted appeals: building fund, welfare fund, international relief.
- Zakat-related handling varies (some distribute, some pass through to partner orgs).
- Trustees must maintain clear records and governance of restricted funds and spending.

## 4) UK Compliance & “Admin Gravity” (Why Software Matters)

### 4.1 Gift Aid: high value but admin-heavy

- Gift Aid generally allows charities to claim **an extra 25p per £1 donated** (from HMRC), but it requires compliant declarations and reporting. [S8], [S9]
- HMRC Charities Online claims:
  - A spreadsheet “schedule” is used for claims, up to **1,000 donations per spreadsheet**, and larger claims require **software**. [S10], [S11]
- Charity CRM vendors (e.g., Beacon) position **direct HMRC submission** + “fully audited” Gift Aid trails as a major advantage. [S12], [S13]

**Implication:** For mosques with high-frequency small donations, Gift Aid can be a large, ongoing “tax rebate” opportunity—but only if the admin is simplified.

### 4.2 GASDS (Gift Aid Small Donations Scheme): uniquely relevant to mosques

- GASDS allows top-up payments on small donations without declarations, but it has constraints like:
  - The **10x rule**: GASDS claim cannot exceed 10x Gift Aid claim in the same tax year. [S14]
  - Community building rules can increase the effective limit per building; guidance exists around per-building claims and local authority collection rules. [S15], [S16]

**Implication:** A mosque-focused system that handles **Gift Aid + GASDS together**, per building, with constraints and reporting baked in, is a strong wedge.

### 4.3 Charity Commission reporting thresholds (impacting software needs)

- Annual return / reporting requirements vary by income thresholds (e.g., under £10k vs £10k–£25k vs over £25k). [S17]
- Receipts & payments (cash accounting) is permitted in England & Wales (for non-company charities) under gross income <= £250k, with reforms proposed/announced to raise certain thresholds over time. [S18], [S19]

**Implication:** Many mosques will be in simpler accounting regimes but still need clean, auditable records and trustee reporting packs.

## 5) Current “Stacks” Used Today (What customers do now)

### 5.1 The default: spreadsheet + donation platform + WhatsApp

Typical tool chain:

- **Google Sheets/Excel** for donors, memberships, rotas, maktab lists.
- **Bank exports** and manual reconciliation.
- Donation platform(s) for online giving.
- **WhatsApp** groups for operational coordination.
- Gift Aid often managed via spreadsheets or partial exports.

Why it persists:

- Lowest cost, low training, volunteer-friendly.
  Where it breaks:
- Fragmented data, poor audit trail, donor comms inconsistent, restricted-fund reporting messy, Gift Aid/GASDS under-claimed.

### 5.2 “Mosque suite” approach (mosque-specific software)

- Attempt to cover donations + memberships + events + sometimes kiosks and apps.
- Often includes donor management and campaign tools.

### 5.3 “Charity CRM” approach (Beacon-like)

- Strong donor CRM + forms + integration ecosystem + Gift Aid automation.
- Usually does **not** cover mosque-specific ops deeply (maktab billing, hall hire, volunteer rotas, facility workflows).

## 6) Competitive Landscape (UK-Relevant)

> Note: These are “market signals” and product positioning as publicly described; verify feature depth via demos.

### 6.1 Beacon CRM (general UK charity CRM, not mosque-specific)

Positioning:

- “All-in-one” charity CRM: donations, supporter management, forms, comms automation.
- Strong emphasis on **Gift Aid automation**, eligibility checking, look-back, and **direct submission to HMRC** with audit trails. [S12], [S13]
- Requires charity HMRC credentials / secure settings to submit claims on behalf of the charity. [S20]

Strengths vs mosque niche:

- Gift Aid workflow depth + UK compliance orientation.
- Modern “system of record” for fundraising.

Gaps for physical-centre mosques:

- Mosque operational modules: maktab fees/attendance, hall bookings, volunteer rotas, on-site donation patterns, per-fund transparency reporting by “Islamic intent”.

### 6.2 MOHID (mosque management ecosystem; UK presence)

Positioning:

- Mosque management + hardware ecosystem (notably donation kiosks).
- Kiosk and cloud integration claims to improve donation capture; supports recurring giving, donor management, campaigns. [S21], [S22]
- Market listings reference modules for membership management, events/programmes, donations at kiosk, and zakat contributions. [S23]

Strengths:

- Mosque-specific workflows; community donation hardware footprint.
- Likely stronger operational “mosque admin” story than generic charity CRMs.

Potential gaps/opportunity space:

- UK-centric Gift Aid + GASDS “done-for-you” flows (verify actual depth).
- Integration ecosystem (accounting, comms, website/CMS) may be less open or less seamless.
- UX simplicity for volunteer treasurers (varies).

### 6.3 Hibabox (mosque/charity donations + kiosk + portal)

Positioning:

- Cloud-based donation management “made for mosque and charities needs”. [S24]
- Offers kiosk/lite devices (contactless) and a portal for running campaigns/pledges. [S25]
- Testimonials mention improved donor pool and multi-campaign operation via portal. [S26]

Strengths:

- On-site giving capture + campaign management; strong fit to physical centres.

Potential gaps/opportunity space:

- How deep it goes beyond fundraising into “centre ops” (bookings, maktab, volunteers).
- UK compliance workflows (Gift Aid/GASDS) depth and auditability (verify).

### 6.4 Masjidbox (global “digital mosque” suite with UK features)

Positioning:

- “All-in-one digital solution” (apps, websites, screens, donations, terminals). [S27]
- Donations product claims **monthly Gift Aid reports for UK** and in-person donation terminal positioning. [S28], [S29]

Strengths:

- Broad digital engagement suite + on-site terminal concept.
- Some UK Gift Aid reporting support.

Potential gaps/opportunity space:

- Whether they provide HMRC direct submission and fully audited Gift Aid/GASDS flows vs “reports only”.
- Depth in finance pack / restricted funds / trustee-ready reporting.

### 6.5 Adjacent fundraising/payment tools (not full CRMs)

- HMRC submission constraints (software required above certain claim size) push organisations toward specialist tools. [S10], [S11]
- Donation terminals/kiosks and “mosque fundraising” content from payment companies (e.g., GoodBox) indicate a competitive attention area for in-person giving. [S30]

## 7) Key Customer Pains (What to build around)

### 7.1 High-value, high-friction pain: Gift Aid + GASDS under-claimed

Root causes:

- Donor declaration capture inconsistent.
- Data scattered across cash logs, terminals, online platforms, bank statements.
- Treasurer role often volunteer; handover risk; time constraints.

### 7.2 Restricted funds & transparency (community trust issue)

- Mosques often run multiple appeals: building fund, welfare, zakat-related, international relief.
- Communities demand clarity: “How much came in, how much spent, what balance remains?”
- Generic CRMs may not model “Islamic intent categories” cleanly and may not produce mosque-friendly reports.

### 7.3 Physical-centre operations are not solved by donation tools

- Hall booking, maktab fees/arrears, event registrations, volunteer rotas.
- Admin lives in WhatsApp + spreadsheets; operational risk and poor audit trail.

### 7.4 Adoption constraints

- Volunteer admins: low training tolerance, change resistance, fear of mistakes.
- Trustees: want governance, approvals, evidence, reporting.
- Staff (if any): need quick UI, minimal clicks, mobile-friendly.

## 8) Product Opportunity: “UK Mosque Ops + Compliance System of Record”

### 8.1 Best initial wedge (strongest ROI)

**A) Donations → Fund allocation → Gift Aid/GASDS → Trustee reporting**

- Build a single ledger that connects:
  - Donation intake (cash batches, terminals, online)
  - Fund categorisation (including restricted/unrestricted and “intent categories”)
  - Gift Aid declarations and eligibility
  - GASDS rules/limits and per-building handling
  - Monthly trustee pack + year-end export for accountant

Why it’s compelling:

- Direct, measurable money impact (reclaimed Gift Aid/GASDS).
- Time savings for treasurers.
- Trust/transparency improvements.

### 8.2 Second wedge (retention/moat)

**Centre operations modules** that don’t exist in charity CRMs:

- Hall booking with deposits/invoices/contracts.
- Maktab registration + recurring billing + arrears + attendance export.
- Volunteer rota/task checklists for Jumu’ah/Ramadan events.

### 8.3 AI opportunities (practical, not gimmicky)

- “Treasurer copilot”: classify transactions into funds; flag anomalies; draft trustee reports.
- Declaration follow-up campaigns: segment donors missing declarations and generate comms.
- Automated reconciliation suggestions: match bank deposits to cash batches/terminal settlements.
  (Keep privacy/security high; maintain audit trail.)

## 9) Segmenting the Market (Who to sell to first)

### Segment 1 — Small mosque (volunteer-led; under 250k income)

Needs:

- Simple, low-cost, minimal training.
- “Do Gift Aid/GASDS properly and give me a monthly report.”

Buyer:

- Treasurer / lead trustee.
  Win condition:
- Demonstrate increased reclaimed income + reduced admin.

### Segment 2 — Mid-size centre (paid admin/caretaker + volunteers)

Needs:

- Multi-role permissions, approvals, operational modules (bookings/maktab), recurring payments.
  Win condition:
- One platform replacing 4–6 tools.

### Segment 3 — Multi-site organisations

Needs:

- Location-level reporting; per-building GASDS; central oversight dashboards.
  Win condition:
- Consolidated reporting and governance.

## 10) Pricing & Packaging Hypotheses (UK Mosque-Realistic)

### Principles

- Many mosques are budget constrained but donation volumes can be high.
- Pricing must map to “fairness”: per-location + transaction tiering often feels acceptable.

Possible packaging:

- **Starter**: donors + funds + receipts + basic Gift Aid pack.
- **Compliance Pro**: HMRC submission + GASDS automation + audit trails + trustee pack.
- **Centre Ops**: bookings + maktab billing + volunteer rota.
- **Enterprise / Multi-site**: consolidation, multi-location, advanced permissions, integrations.

Add-ons:

- Terminal/kiosk integrations (or own hardware partner).
- SMS/WhatsApp messaging integration.

## 12) Competitive Strategy: Where to Win vs MOHID/Hibabox/Masjidbox/Beacon

### 12.1 Compete on “UK compliance depth + auditability + reporting”

- Beacon sets expectation for automated HMRC submissions and auditing. [S12], [S13]
- Mosque suites often compete on kiosk/ops breadth; you can win by being:
  - _the_ UK-compliance-first + finance-reporting-first platform for mosques.

### 12.2 Differentiate on “mosque-centre transparency model”

- First-class “fund intent categories” (Zakat/Sadaqah/Lillah/Building/Welfare/etc.).
- Trustee-friendly “fund balance” reporting and donation attribution.

### 12.3 Own “physical-centre operational workflows”

- Booking + maktab + volunteer rotas integrated with donor/payment ledger.
- Mosque suites have some of this; differentiation comes from simplicity and UK reporting.

## 13) Risks & Constraints

### 13.1 Competition exists (not a greenfield)

- MOHID, Hibabox, Masjidbox explicitly target mosques with donation and/or operational tooling. [S21]–[S29]
  **Mitigation:** pick a sharp wedge (Gift Aid/GASDS + funds reporting + trustee packs) and integrate where needed.

### 13.2 Data privacy & safeguarding data

- If you handle safeguarding notes or sensitive welfare cases, data sensitivity increases substantially.
  **Mitigation:** keep MVP away from sensitive case data; offer “safeguarding incident logging” only with strong controls and minimal data.

### 13.3 Integration complexity (terminals, banks, accounting)

- Terminal settlement matching and bank reconciliation can be deceptively hard.
  **Mitigation:** start with CSV import + reconciliation assist; build direct integrations iteratively.

### MVP v1: “Money + compliance + reporting”

1. Donor/supporter CRM + receipts

3. Funds/intent ledger: restricted/unrestricted + categories
4. Gift Aid: declaration capture + eligibility + claim packs + audit trail
5. GASDS: rules/limits + per-building support (where applicable)
6. Monthly trustee pack + accountant export (CSV)

### v1.5/v2: “Centre ops”

7. Bookings: calendar + deposits + invoices
8. Maktab: enrolment + recurring billing + arrears
9. Volunteer rota + checklists
10. Comms: email/SMS/WhatsApp integrations

## 15) Research-to-Product Summary (1-liner)

**There is a credible UK opportunity for a mosque-centre SaaS that combines “donation reality (cash/contactless/online)” with “UK compliance (Gift Aid + GASDS)” and “trustee-ready fund transparency,” then expands into bookings/maktab/volunteer ops — competing less on hardware and more on compliance depth + auditability + reporting simplicity.**
